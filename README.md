# AI Video & Multimodal Evaluation

A professional portfolio documenting practical experience and evaluation methodologies for AI-generated and real-world video data.

This repository focuses on the structured evaluation of visual content across multiple dimensions, including visual quality, prompt alignment, physical plausibility, motion quality, and temporal consistency.

---

## Overview

AI-generated video systems can produce visually convincing content while still containing subtle errors in motion, object interactions, physical behavior, temporal continuity, or alignment with the intended prompt.

This project demonstrates a structured approach to identifying, analyzing, and documenting these issues.

The evaluation process combines visual inspection, temporal analysis, critical reasoning, and evidence-based reporting.

---

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Visual Quality

Evaluates the overall visual presentation and technical quality of the video.

Areas of assessment include:

* Image clarity and sharpness
* Resolution and visible detail
* Object and scene rendering
* Lighting and visual artifacts
* Facial and object appearance quality
* Unwanted blur, pixelation, or visual degradation

---

### 2. Prompt Alignment

Determines whether the generated video accurately represents the requested scenario.

Areas of assessment include:

* Presence of requested objects
* Correct actions and activities
* Scene and environmental requirements
* Character or subject attributes
* Correct sequence of requested events
* Overall adherence to the intended prompt

---

### 3. Physical Plausibility

Evaluates whether objects, people, and environments behave in a physically believable manner.

Areas of assessment include:

* Object interactions
* Human anatomy and movement
* Gravity and contact
* Object permanence
* Spatial relationships
* Realistic physical behavior

---

### 4. Motion Quality

Evaluates the quality and realism of movement throughout the video.

Areas of assessment include:

* Smoothness of motion
* Natural movement trajectories
* Acceleration and deceleration
* Object and character movement
* Camera movement
* Unnatural or inconsistent motion

---

### 5. Temporal Consistency

Evaluates whether visual elements remain consistent throughout the sequence.

Areas of assessment include:

* Object identity and appearance
* Character consistency
* Scene continuity
* Position and orientation
* Lighting continuity
* State changes across frames
* Continuity of actions and events

---

## Evaluation Methodology

The evaluation process follows a structured workflow:

**Observe → Identify → Locate → Categorize → Assess → Document**

### Observe

Review the video carefully to understand the scene, actions, objects, and overall visual behavior.

### Identify

Detect visible inconsistencies, quality problems, or deviations from the intended scenario.

### Locate

Determine where the issue occurs within the video and identify the relevant segment or temporal location.

### Categorize

Assign the issue to the appropriate evaluation dimension.

### Assess

Determine the severity and significance of the issue based on its impact on the overall video.

### Document

Record a concise, evidence-based description that clearly explains what happened and where it occurred.

---

## Severity Framework

### Major

Issues that substantially affect the video's realism, usability, or adherence to the intended scenario.

Examples include:

* Major physical impossibilities
* Significant prompt failures
* Severe temporal inconsistencies
* Major anatomical distortions
* Critical object or scene changes

### Moderate

Noticeable issues that affect quality or realism but do not completely undermine the video.

Examples include:

* Inconsistent object behavior
* Noticeable motion problems
* Moderate visual artifacts
* Partial prompt deviations
* Continuity problems

### Minor

Small imperfections that are noticeable but have limited impact on the overall result.

Examples include:

* Slight visual softness
* Small continuity inconsistencies
* Minor motion irregularities
* Subtle rendering artifacts

---

## Evidence-Based Evaluation

A strong evaluation should describe the observable evidence rather than relying on assumptions.

Each finding should answer three questions:

**What happened?**

Describe the visible issue.

**Where did it happen?**

Identify the relevant segment or point in the video.

**Why does it matter?**

Explain how the issue affects visual quality, prompt alignment, physical plausibility, motion, or temporal consistency.

Example:

> During the later portion of the sequence, an object changes position without a visible interaction causing the movement. This creates a temporal and physical continuity issue because the object's state is inconsistent between consecutive moments.

---

## Evaluation Principles

This portfolio follows several core principles:

* Evaluate only what is visibly supported by the video.
* Avoid inventing or assuming events that cannot be observed.
* Distinguish visual quality problems from physical or anatomical problems.
* Consider the complete temporal sequence rather than isolated frames.
* Use precise object names and observable actions.
* Categorize each issue according to its primary failure mode.
* Provide concise, evidence-based explanations.
* Consider severity based on the impact of the issue.
